# Prigl tone: the craft without the cynicism

Prigl (prigl.cz) is a Brno satirical blog. Its craft is excellent and transferable. Its attitude
(swearing, contempt, despair as a punchline) is not. This skill keeps the first and drops the
second. The default reader is "Petr", an electrician from Nový Jičín: clever, busy, allergic to
preaching and to big words. Write so that he nods, smiles once per paragraph and reads to the end.

## 1. The core in five lines

1. **Start in the middle.** The first sentence is a voice, a picture or a claim you can disagree
   with. Never a warm-up, never "v dnešní době".
2. **Name it, price it, time it.** A place, a number and an object you can hold do the work that
   adjectives and verdicts do in weaker texts.
3. **Long sentence, short blow.** A sentence that piles up detail, then two to five words that
   land it. The blow closes the paragraph.
4. **Big word on a small thing.** The humour comes from the seam between a solemn register and a
   kettle, a queue, a staircase.
5. **The author sits at the same table.** The narrator is always among those being laughed at,
   and the text ends without a sermon.

## 2. Openers

Pick one type per piece. The examples are new; do not copy Prigl's.

- **Direct speech first, speaker and place after.** The reader hears a voice before knowing the
  topic; the reporting clause carries the detail.
  „Tak to zkus vypnout a zapnout,“ radí Petr sousedce ve čtvrtém patře a už ví, že na oběd
  přijde o hodinu později.
- **Exact time and place, two bare sentences.** Like a stage direction.
  Sobota, 6.40, parkoviště u Kauflandu. Rohlíky jsou ještě teplé, lidi ještě ne.
- **A thesis that provokes, as short as possible.** Stated as a fact, without "myslíme si".
  Nejtěžší věta v češtině má dvě slova. Potřebuju pomoct.
- **A big truth dropped onto a small place.** Sentence one is solemn, sentence two is local.
  Člověk není stvořený k samotě. Na zastávce se to pozná ve čtvrt na šest ráno, když jedenáct
  lidí mlčí každý do svého telefonu.
- **A common idea, corrected.** "Někdo si myslí… My…"
  Někdo si myslí, že do kostela se chodí v obleku a s vyřešeným životem. My chodíme v mikině
  a s otázkami.
- **Then and now.**
  Dřív jsme si přes plot půjčovali vajíčka. Dnes si přes plot půjčujeme heslo na wifi.
- **"Ať už… nebo…"**: two concrete opposites and one common denominator.
  Ať už jste se brali na zámku, nebo na úřadě ve čtvrtek v jedenáct, v úterý večer řešíte
  totéž: kdo vynese koš.

What they share: a person, a place or a number is in the first sentence, and nothing announces
what the text will be about.

## 3. Structure

A short piece (one manifesto value, 80–200 words) runs in four steps:

1. **Opener** (one or two sentences, see above).
2. **Scene**: one person, one object, one line of speech. Present tense. The person is fixed by
   role, age and one prop, not by a verdict ("Petr, elektrikář", "paní z přízemí s jezevčíkem").
3. **The straight sentence**: one plain statement of what we believe or do, with no irony in it.
   The scene has earned it, so it can be short. Keep it visibly apart from the jokes (its own
   sentence, often its own paragraph), so the reader never has to guess what is meant seriously.
4. **The ending**: a short blow that opens a door.

Longer pieces repeat a micro-pattern inside each section: **claim → mini-scene with one line of
speech → one dry sentence**. Subheadings may be small jokes themselves, best as a parallel row
(„Kdo nosí židle“, „Kdo nosí koláče“, „Kdo nosí výmluvy“).

**How to end.** Never a summary, never a moral. Stop one paragraph earlier than a school essay
would. Working types:

- a concrete detail instead of a conclusion: „Kafe bylo. Bábovka taky.“
- the last word goes to somebody else, quoted as said: „Tak zase v neděli,“ řekl a vzal si
  ještě jeden koláč na cestu.
- the opening motif returns, shifted (the washing machine from sentence one becomes a wardrobe);
- the turn to ourselves: „Zatím nám to jde střídavě. Ale zkoušíme to každý týden.“
- an invitation that costs the reader nothing: „Židle navíc tu stojí pořád.“

Prigl often ends in resignation ("it's all pointless, and that's the charm"). Keep the brevity,
reverse the direction: the last sentence carries warmth, hope or an invitation.

## 4. Rhythm

- **The long sentence** has 25–45 words, gathers details in one breath and ends on its most
  concrete word, an object or a number.
- **The blow** has 2–5 words. It is a flat statement, not an exclamation: „Přišli všichni.“
  „Vydrželo to dodnes.“ „Nikdo se neptal.“ Prigl's own blows work the same way without a single
  rude word: „Nikoho to nezajímá.“ „To je všechno.“
- Roughly **three long to one short**. Two blows in a row only as a deliberate refrain.
- The blow sits **at the end of a paragraph**, or at its start as a new claim. Never in the middle.
- **Paragraphs have 2–5 sentences**, one picture each. A one-sentence paragraph is allowed as a
  hinge.
- Use **triples and parallel sentences** with one word swapped („Kdo má auto, veze. Kdo má
  čas, nese. Kdo nemá ani jedno, drží dveře.“), anaphora, and one-word transitions at the start
  of a paragraph („Jenže.“ „Každopádně.“).
- **No exclamation marks** outside direct speech. Humour is never flagged.
- A number or a time may be a sentence: „Sedmdesát kilo.“ „Půl šesté.“

## 5. Concrete detail

Every claim gets anchored. Use one or two kinds per paragraph, not all at once:

- **Place by the name locals use**: a street, a stop, a shop, a hill, a neighbouring village.
  For Nový Jičín that means things like Masarykovo náměstí, Skalky, Čerťák, Svinec, Kaufland,
  the morning shift at the factory, Šenov, Kopřivnice. **Use only real names you can verify**;
  if unsure, use a true generic anchor (ve čtvrtém patře bez výtahu) rather than a made-up one.
- **Price, count, weight, time**: „kafe za 39“, „pět pater“, „6.40“, „třetí čtvrtek v měsíci“.
  A small number next to a big word is already a joke.
- **An object you can pick up**: vodováha, termoska, klíče od sklepa, plech s buchtami.
- **Role, age, one prop** instead of a character sketch: „dvaačtyřicetiletý elektrikář
  s čelovkou, kterou si zapomněl sundat“.
- **A small gesture with the line of speech**: the speaker is always doing something.
- **One object that does not belong** and is therefore remembered.

Put the detail at the end of the sentence. Its job: prove the author was there, let the reader
say "to znám", and replace judgement.

**Banned generic words and what to write instead**

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| v dnešní uspěchané době | v úterý v půl šesté, když zavírá školka |
| mnoho lidí, každý z nás | Petr; paní z přízemí; jedenáct lidí na zastávce |
| společenství, vztahy | dvanáct lidí u jednoho stolu a třináctá židle |
| pomoc bližnímu, podpora | odvézt sousedku na rentgen; vynést pračku do pátého patra |
| přijetí, otevřená náruč | nikdo se neptá, kde jsi byl celý rok |
| důležitý, klíčový, hluboký | delete; show what it costs (sobotu, dvě hodiny, tři stovky) |
| často, někdy, brzy | každý čtvrtek; třikrát za zimu; ve 5.40 |
| levný, drahý | the price |
| různé aktivity | name three; the third one surprises |

## 6. Humour techniques that survive without cynicism

- **Hyperbole.** Exaggerate one thing and report it as a dry fact, in an official voice.
  Na brigádu přišlo tolik lidí, že na jedny hrábě připadali tři poradci.
- **High style on a low thing.** Describe a household object in the language of office, science
  or liturgy; the object must be ours and the tone visibly tender.
  Konvice ve sborové kuchyňce zastává úřad, na který by jinde potřebovali tajemníka: svolává
  lidi, uklidňuje je a nikomu neskáče do řeči.
- **False precision.** Put an exact number where nobody counts. Only on harmless things (minutes,
  stairs, cakes), never as a fake study or expert.
  Petr slíbil, že se zastaví na pět minut. Odešel za dvě hodiny a čtyři koláče.
- **A list with a twist at the end.** Three items of one kind, the last from another world.
  Přinesli jsme vrtačku, hmoždinky, vodováhu a dobrou vůli. Chyběl jen někdo, kdo ví, kudy vedou
  dráty.
- **A quote "from the pub".** A short line exactly as people say it, without correction. The
  speaker must come out wiser or kinder than expected; use a neutral reporting verb and let the
  line work alone.
  „Modlit se neumím, ale lešení vám postavím,“ řekl soused. A postavil.
- **Self-irony.** The "we" is the first target; admit a small, specific weakness.
  Zpíváme rádi a nahlas. Čistě zpívá Jana, my ostatní jí děláme křoví.
- **Ironic naming of a phenomenon.** Coin one term for a habit we all share, then use it as if it
  were an established discipline.
  Říkáme tomu parkovištní spiritualita: nejupřímnější rozhovory začínají až u auta, s klíčky
  v ruce.
- **Understatement and the dry bracket.** A factual aside that delivers the point quietly.
  Topení v sále jsme si opravili sami (dvakrát).

One or two techniques per paragraph. If every sentence performs, none of them lands.

## 7. Register and vocabulary

- **Narrator: standard Czech that sounds spoken.** Short main clauses, verbs instead of nouns,
  active voice, everyday words. Colloquial vocabulary is welcome (kafe, šichta, panelák,
  brigáda, mobil), colloquial endings are not.
- **Direct speech: as people really talk.** There the endings may go colloquial („To je
  dobrý.“ „Já bych to nechal bejt.“) and a local word may appear. Use only forms people around
  Nový Jičín actually say; Brno words (šalina, hantec) are wrong there. One or two such words per
  piece are seasoning, more is a costume.
- **One bookish word** (nýbrž, pročež, jal se) next to a homely one is allowed now and then for
  comic height. Not in the straight sentence.
- **Address**: "my" that includes the writer's own faults; the reader as plural "vy", described
  inside a situation rather than commanded. Conspiratorial asides in small doses: „Znáte to.“
  „Ruku na srdce.“
- **Avoid**: English jargon, church jargon (obecenství, služebnost, požehnaný čas) unless quoted
  with a smile at ourselves, diminutives used as mockery or as sweetness (kafíčko, človíček),
  ironic quotation marks, nominal chains, passive voice.
- Local names create belonging, not a gate: a newcomer must still understand the sentence.

## 8. What NOT to take

**Rule: we laugh at ourselves and our habits, never at others. Every joke ends in warmth or
hope.**

- **Swearing and its hints**: no vulgar words, no asterisks, no rhymes or dots that let the
  reader fill one in. Where Prigl's blow is a rude word, use a flat factual sentence.
- **Cynicism and nihilism**: no "stejně je to jedno", no despair as a punchline, no irony that
  leaves the reader with nothing.
- **Contempt and punching down**: no jokes about someone being poor, old, fat, slow, rural,
  uneducated, drunk or ill; no labels for groups (women, men, minorities, Prague, the next
  town, other churches, non-believers); no nicknames based on a flaw. A nickname by role is fine.
- **Typologies of "them"**. A type may appear only if the writer and the reader gladly recognise
  themselves in it („my, co přijdeme vždycky o pět minut později“).
- **A common enemy as glue.** Belonging comes from shared places and habits, not from who is out.
- **Quotes that expose the speaker**, mocking reporting verbs, invented quotes from real named
  people, fake experts and studies.
- **Shock material**: violence, sex, addiction, illness, death or bodily functions as a source
  of comparison.
- **Irony about what we mean**: never ironic about faith, hope, Scripture or the reader's
  sincerity. No ironic praise; when the text praises, it means it.
- **Alcohol as the shared code.** Replace the pub with the stop, the queue, the garden, Sunday
  lunch, the stairwell.
- **A parody held for the whole text.** One borrowed form in one place is enough.

Test for every joke: who is smaller after it? Only "we" is an acceptable answer.

## 9. Checklist

Answer yes to each before handing the text over.

1. Does the first sentence contain a voice, a picture or a claim, and no warm-up?
2. Is there at least one named place, one number and one object you can hold?
3. Did you verify every real name, or replace it with a true generic anchor?
4. Does every paragraph end on its strongest, shortest sentence?
5. Is there a 2–5 word blow after every two to four long sentences?
6. Do paragraphs stay within 2–5 sentences, one picture each?
7. Are all the words from the banned table gone?
8. Is the butt of every joke "we" or our habit, never another person or group?
9. Is there at least one sentence said straight, clearly apart from the jokes?
10. Does the ending carry warmth, hope or an invitation, without summing up or preaching?
11. Is the text free of swearing, hints at swearing and ironic quotation marks?
12. Are colloquial endings only inside direct speech?
13. Would Petr read it aloud to a colleague without embarrassment?
14. Is the Czech natural: verbs, active voice, no calques, no church or office jargon?
15. Are there no more than two humour techniques per paragraph?

## 10. Before and after

**Before (flat church prose)**

> Jako společenství věříme, že je důležité vytvářet prostředí vzájemné podpory a přijetí.
> V dnešní uspěchané době má mnoho lidí pocit osamělosti, a proto chceme být místem, kde může
> každý najít otevřenou náruč a kde jsou budovány hluboké vztahy. Pomoc bližnímu vnímáme jako
> jednu z našich klíčových hodnot.

**After**

> „Až tu pračku vyneseme, dáš si kafe?“ ptá se Petr v sobotu v osm ráno pod schody paneláku,
> kde výtah nejede od čtvrtka. Pračka váží sedmdesát kilo, pater je pět a paní, které patří, se
> omlouvá na každém odpočívadle. Nahoře jsme zpocení, zadýchaní a jeden z nás tvrdí, že má něco
> se zády, přestože nesl jen hadici. Kafe bylo. Bábovka taky.
>
> Věříme, že nám Bůh dal sousedy schválně. Proto pomáháme, i když se to nehodí, a necháme si
> pomoct, i když je to těžší.
>
> Příští sobotu se stěhuje skříň. Přijďte, schodů je dost pro všechny.

What changed: direct speech opens; numbers replace abstractions; the joke is on one of us; the
belief stands alone in two plain sentences; the ending returns to the opening motif and invites.
